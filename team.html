Amédée des Georges

Principal Investigator

Associate Professor, Department of Molecular Pathobiology, NYU College of Dentistry

Amédée studies how biological macromolecules work and how their activity is regulated. His lab combines cryo-electron microscopy, biophysics, and computational approaches to connect molecular structure and dynamics with function, with the goal of understanding disease and helping develop new treatments.

He trained in biochemistry at Université Pierre et Marie Curie in Paris and earned his PhD at the University of Cambridge, working with Linda Amos at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology. He then pursued postdoctoral research with Joachim Frank at Columbia University. He established his independent laboratory at CUNY in 2015 and moved the lab to NYU in 2023.

Amédée aims to build a collaborative lab where scientists exchange ideas, think creatively, and develop their independence.

Harsh Bansia

Postdoctoral Scientist

Harsh combines cryo-electron microscopy, molecular dynamics simulations, and computational analysis to investigate how protein structure and motion determine function. He earned his BSc and MSc in Biotechnology at Jiwaji University and his PhD in Structural and Computational Biology at the Indian Institute of Science. His doctoral work explored hidden allosteric features of proteins and their implications for drug design. He joined the lab in 2020.

Andres Cabezas

PhD Student

CUNY Graduate Center

Andres studies how interactions between proteins and lipids influence membrane protein function, using cryo-electron microscopy to examine large protein complexes such as calcium channels. He earned his BS in Biology at New Jersey City University in 2019, where he gained research experience in Yufeng Wei’s biochemistry lab. Jack Mechler

PhD Student

CUNY Graduate Center

Co-mentored with Pinar Ayata

Jack uses cryo-electron microscopy and computational methods to study membrane proteins in their native environments, with an interest in the structural biology of Alzheimer’s disease. He studied at St. Lawrence University, where he investigated bacterial amyloid proteins in the Marano lab. His doctoral work is jointly mentored by Amédée des Georges and Pinar Ayata. Mitali Bhate

PhD Student

CUNY Graduate Center

Co-mentored with Prabodhika Mallikaratchy

Mitali studies membrane protein interactions, with a particular interest in orphan G protein-coupled receptors implicated in neurodegenerative disease. Her work draws on cell and molecular biology, protein biochemistry, and cryo-electron microscopy to understand these receptors in cellular settings. Her collaborative research also includes aptamers that recognize native GPCRs. Outside the lab, she enjoys reading, hiking, and exploring wildlife and aquatic life.
